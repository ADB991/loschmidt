Loschmidt

Every rule here is a bijection on configurations, so one time slice fixes the whole history, past and future. The board is a torus. Click a cell anywhere in space-time and the history is rewritten in both directions.

What to try

  • Thermalisation. Start from a low-entropy pattern and watch the coarse-grained entropy climb to its typical value. Entropy grows in both time directions away from t = 0. Start from a random state and nothing visible happens.
  • Typicality depends on the rule. Particle-conserving rules thermalise at fixed density. Q2R conserves the Ising energy, so its equilibrium keeps a fixed density of domain walls. Free and integrable rules (swap gas, diagonal gas, RCA54, box-ball) never reach the iid values.
  • Ergodicity. Compare a cell's time average with the space average now. Probes (⌖) follow single cells; the histogram shows every cell's time average. For a mixing rule the spread shrinks like 1/√T.
  • Loschmidt echo. Encode a pattern, scramble it forwards, flip one cell, run back. The damage at t = 0 is the backward light cone of the flip: small after a short scramble, everywhere after a long one. In linear rules it is a sparse fractal, so the pattern survives.
  • Light cone and damage. ◎ Light cone shades the causal light cone of your last toggle: every cell whose value could depend on it under this rule. While it is on, each toggle is compared with the history just before it, and in 1D hovering previews the next one. ◐ Damage colours the cells that actually changed (red: on now, off in the reference; blue: the reverse). Their front moves at the butterfly speed, which can be slower than the light cone. The two switch independently.
  • Unfolding. In 1D, an edit does not appear everywhere at once: the new history spreads outwards from the edited row at the play speed, so you can follow its consequences into the future and the past.
  • Recurrence. The board is finite, so every orbit is periodic. Small boards and linear rules come back quickly.
